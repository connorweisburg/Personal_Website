---
title: On progress trackers
date: 2026-06-03
tags: [systems, metro, productivity]
excerpt: Building a single-source progress tracker to manage fragmented program updates.
---

The first big problem I identified within the continuity program at Metro was a lack of progress tracking. Nobody knew the state of all of the plans. There was no way to monitor the program and gauge its status without having to go into all of the plans themselves.

This caused a lot of confusion, headache, and inconsistencies across plans because that information is too complex to be held in one head. Tasks were slipping through the cracks left and right.

I needed a central source of knowledge about what year each plan was updated most recently, what the update had looked like, whether it was still pending, and how finished it was.

In response to this, I designed a progress tracker in a spreadsheet that listed all plans, who was involved, status (not started, in progress, completed — which eventually turned into a percentage), and notes that listed recent tasks and other significant information.

Now, in just one screen, it was possible to analyze the whole program.

This sped things up.

From a bird’s-eye view, you can see the next “worm.” From a progress tracking view, you can see the next email to write. Quickly, without having to dig around in the dirt.

10/10 system.

I use something similar to keep track of music production — just songs and recordings instead of plans, and musicians/singers instead of a planning team.
